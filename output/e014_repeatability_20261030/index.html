What the two response views and statistics mean

Waveform-specific deviation is the context/time/position-adjusted waveform effect from E014, with the common waveform response removed. It asks whether a distinguishing waveform deviation repeats.

Total observed response retains the entire response for that waveform, giving its observed comparison cells equal weight. A repeatable total response can be shared by every waveform, or reflect repeatable artifacts/context. It is not evidence of waveform identity information.

At a single electrode × bin, each half supplies one number: a correlation is undefined. The heatmap instead shows that waveform's mean A×B product across 100 splits, signed full-data response, RMS half-to-half disagreement or fraction of splits with matching signs.

Temporal pattern r compares one feature's held-out A/B time courses within the declared one-second support; joint pattern r combines features and time. Sensor and power maps retain scalp electrodes. Power uses eleven overlapping 500-ms windows whose centres run from 300 to 800 ms. xDAWN uses ten learned components, not ten fixed electrodes.

Calibration/A/B comparisons: xDAWN fits only independent calibration trials. Its 62- and 14-sensor controls use exactly the same A/B trials and calibration-only RMS normalization. Joint shape r demeans each feature's time course before flattening. Compare these three matched representations to each other; their normalization, trial counts and joint-r definition differ from the two-way sensor/power views.

Voltage/derivative/power features use the original E011 paired splits and 595 pairs. xDAWN uses 100 calibration/A/B allocations, with disjoint complete pairs. Two-pair strata supply A/B only. Component class/rank names change spatial meaning across calibration fits and are not fixed brain sources. The selected method box explains each representation. All uncertainty is exploratory; no classifier or significant-feature selection is performed.
